Cottage business

From hobby batches to a real cottage business

Freeze-dried candy and snacks are among the most popular cottage food products in the country — and among the easiest to get wrong legally. This hub collects everything we publish on selling what you dry.

Before anything else: cottage food rules are state law, they vary widely, and they change. Everything here is educational. Confirm your state's current rules with your Department of Agriculture or health department before your first sale.

The three questions every new seller must answer

1. Is my product allowed? Most states allow shelf-stable, non-potentially-hazardous foods under cottage law. For freeze-dried products the dividing line is usually water activity — 0.85 or below is the common threshold — plus categorical rules (meat, poultry, and seafood are broadly excluded).

2. Can I prove it? A growing number of states expect sellers to verify water activity. University extension programs have validated sub-$100 meters for cottage producers; our economics guide covers testing practice and record-keeping.

3. Does the math work? Electricity, packaging, absorbers, labels, candy cost, and — above all — your machine's cycle time set a floor under your pricing. Log a few real batches in the Batch Log before you print price tags.
